# LinkedIn: ai-agent-or-automation

## Post 1: native text

Most processes people want to "put AI on" need a lot less AI than the pitch suggests.

If you can write the steps down and they come out the same every time, that's automation. An AI agent earns its place only when the next step depends on reading something messy and deciding what to do about it.

Say a property manager wants AI for maintenance requests. Broken into parts:

• Logging the request and confirming receipt is automation.
• Turning "the thing under the sink is dripping again" into "plumbing, low urgency, unit 4B" is where a model helps.
• Picking a vendor by trade and building is a rule.
• Booking the visit and telling the tenant is automation.
• Deciding whether a burst pipe at 2 a.m. needs an emergency plumber stays with a person.

That's one AI step inside an ordinary workflow. Calling it an agent would add cost and unpredictability without changing the result.

An agent makes sense when each case takes a different path: check the vendor's reply, reschedule when they decline, ask the tenant for photos, decide when to escalate.

A useful exercise before building anything: write the process down, mark each step "rule" or "judgment," and circle the judgment steps where a mistake would be expensive. The rules are your automation. The circled steps are where a person keeps the final say.
