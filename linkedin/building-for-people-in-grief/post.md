## Post 1: native text

The people using this software arrive on one of the worst days of their year.

A small nonprofit we built for supports women through the loss of a parent. Its members come to it grieving, often on a phone, sometimes without much patience for technology. That shaped the build more than any feature list did.

The application asks one group of questions at a time, so nobody faces a long form on a bad day. A memorial wish offers a few preset phrases before it offers an empty text box. Nothing important happens on a single click without a chance to stop.

The anniversary of a parent's death is recorded as a Hebrew date, so it moves on the ordinary calendar every year. The memorial wall works it out fresh each year, leap-year rule included, and sends reminders before the day.

A member's record belongs to her. Closing an account is something she asks for, and erasing her details later leaves the memorial to her parent in place.

The nonprofit has no engineers on staff, so wherever we could, the software enforces its own rules.

If you're building for people who arrive on a hard day, start by asking who they are and what state they're in.
