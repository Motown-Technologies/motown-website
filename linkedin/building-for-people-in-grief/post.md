## Post 1: native text

People who come to a grief-support organization are there for the help. The software is just the way in, and every question it asks is one more thing to do on a day when they already have plenty to carry.

A few things we'd check in any product built for people going through something hard:

• Ask for less, in smaller pieces. One group of questions at a time feels manageable, especially on a phone.
• Give people words to start from. When members of one nonprofit we worked with leave a memorial wish for someone, the app offers a few preset phrases first, and they can still write their own.
• Write down the tones to avoid. That nonprofit's design brief named four: clinical, corporate, greeting-card and funereal.
• Get the dates that matter exactly right. Their members mark a parent's anniversary on the Hebrew calendar, so it moves every year and has to be worked out fresh.
• Treat each member's record as hers. Closing an account is her request, and erasing her details later leaves the memorial to her parent in place.
• Let the software keep the rules a small team can't watch every week.

Most of these decisions come from one question: who arrives at your product, and what kind of day are they having?
