# LinkedIn: core-web-vitals-explained

## Post 1: native text

Core Web Vitals are three things Google measures from real visits to your site, and each one is something your visitors already feel.

LCP is how long the main content takes to appear. Good is 2.5 seconds or less.

INP is how quickly the page reacts to a tap or click. Good is 200 milliseconds or less. It replaced an older metric, First Input Delay, in 2024.

CLS is how much the layout jumps while it loads. Good is 0.1 or less.

Google takes these from real Chrome users and looks at the 75th percentile, so roughly three in four visits need to hit the target. Your laptop on office wifi is the best case. Your customers may be on a mid-range phone on mobile data.

On rankings, Google's own guidance is modest. Good scores don't guarantee a top position, and Search still prefers the most relevant page even when its experience is worse. Speed matters most for what visitors do once they've arrived.

The usual causes are familiar: oversized images, too much third-party JavaScript, and ads or banners that push content around. Most sites also get slower over time as widgets and tracking tags pile up. Checking your scores in PageSpeed Insights or Search Console now and then catches the slide while it's still a small fix.
