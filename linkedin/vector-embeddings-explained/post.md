## Post 1: native text

Your refund policy is written down. Your company search still can't find it, because the customer typed "how do I get my money back."

Keyword search matches words, and those two share none. Vector embeddings let software search by meaning. A model turns each passage into a long list of numbers that works like a spot on a map, so "terminate the agreement" lands near "cancel the contract." Your question becomes numbers too, and search returns whatever's nearest.

That's the search half of most AI assistants built on company documents. Know its limits before you buy one:

• It's bad at exact strings. To the model, part HX-4410-B looks almost the same as HX-4410-C. Running keyword search alongside it helps.
• It always returns something, even when nothing relevant exists, so it needs a way to say it came up empty.
• If the HR folder is indexed, anyone who can ask a question can surface it, unless access is checked on every search.

Before you talk to a vendor, collect a few dozen searches your team gave up on, plus the passages they should have found. Include part numbers or policy codes if you use them, and test every version against that list.
