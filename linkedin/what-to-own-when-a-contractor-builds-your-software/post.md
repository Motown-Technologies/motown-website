# LinkedIn: what-to-own-when-a-contractor-builds-your-software

## Post 1: native text

Before a contractor writes any code for you, open the accounts in your company's name and give them access you can take away later.

Most ownership problems are set up in week one, when it's quickest for the developer to use their own accounts. Nobody notices until the contractor leaves, a payment is disputed, or an investor asks who owns the code.

Start with copyright. Paying for software doesn't automatically make it yours. Under US law, commissioned work only counts as "work made for hire" in nine listed categories, with a signed agreement, and custom software often doesn't fit. That's why contracts usually include a clause assigning the IP to you. Check yours is signed and covers subcontractors, and have a lawyer look at it.

The rest is mostly accounts:

• The code repository, in your company's GitHub or GitLab organization, with its full history.
• Hosting, billed to your card, with the contractor as a user you can remove.
• The domain, registered to your company. Your email probably depends on it.
• Payment, email and app store accounts, with keys in a password manager you control.

One thing to check this week: look up your domain and see whose name is on it. If it isn't your company's, fix it while the person who registered it still answers your emails.
