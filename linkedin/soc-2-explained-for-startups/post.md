# LinkedIn: soc-2-explained-for-startups

## Post 1: native text

If a prospect's security team asks for your SOC 2 report and you don't have one, don't promise a date on that call. The version most buyers want takes months of evidence.

SOC 2 is an audit report on your controls (the checks you say you run), written by a licensed CPA firm. Nobody gets certified. The report describes your system and records what the auditor tested and found.

There are two types. Type 1 checks your controls are designed properly at one point in time. Type 2 asks whether they worked throughout a period, often several months. Say you review access every quarter: the auditor wants every review in that window, dated and signed off. Miss one and the report lists an exception.

Most enterprise buyers want Type 2, and that's where startups get caught. A deal hangs on the report and the observation period hasn't even started.

Two other mistakes are common. Template policies describe a company you aren't, and every policy you don't follow becomes a finding. And buyers usually want a fresh report each year, so the controls have to keep running.

When a prospect asks, find out which type and scope they need, and by when. Compare that with the evidence you already have. If the dates don't fit, say so early and ask what their security team can review in the meantime.
