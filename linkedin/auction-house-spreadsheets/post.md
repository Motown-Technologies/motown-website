## Post 1: native text

An auction house we built for ran its inventory on spreadsheets, a shared photo folder and a group chat.

It worked until two people disagreed about where a lot stood, and none of the three could settle it.

A spreadsheet can tell you where an item is, if someone remembered to update it. It can't tell you who changed it or when. Keep the same status in three places and they drift apart.

So we built one system of record, from intake to catalog to auction:

• Status comes from the record itself, so every screen shows the same answer.
• Every change is recorded against the person who made it, and deletes can be undone.
• An AI model drafts the catalog copy from the photos. A cataloger reviews and approves every draft before it reaches the inventory.
• Photos upload straight from a phone on the warehouse floor.

The AI part is what we'd recommend to anyone adding it to an internal process. Let the model do the tedious first pass and keep the decision with a person.

If your team runs on spreadsheets and the same status lives in more than one place, it's probably time for a single record.
