An item could be "photographed" in the spreadsheet, "still in storage" in the group chat, and have two differently named photos in the shared folder. At an auction house we worked with, nobody was being careless. Each tool held part of the inventory process, and working out which version to trust had become a job of its own.

## Three tools gave three answers

The spreadsheet could tell you where an item was, as long as someone remembered to update it. It couldn't explain why the chat said something else. A naming rule could help someone find a photo, but it couldn't change the item's status in the chat. Each tool worked on its own. The trouble was knowing whether the person making the next decision had the current information.

Tidier tabs, color coding and stricter naming help for a while. The same fact is still stored in more than one place, by different people, and the copies drift.

## We gave every item one record

We replaced the three with one record per item that follows it from intake through photography, cataloging and sale. The status is worked out from what has actually happened to the item, so every screen and report gives the same answer.

The new system also records who changed an item, and deleted records come back from a recently-deleted screen. With the spreadsheet, getting a row back meant restoring an older file and losing everyone's later edits.

## The typing went to software, and the decisions stayed with people

While we were in there, we looked at the repetitive work around the spreadsheet. Catalogers used to write every title and description by hand. Now an AI model drafts them from the photos, and a cataloger reads, corrects and approves every draft before it goes into the inventory.

## What we'd tell another team

You don't need to run an auction house to have this problem. These are the signs we'd look for:

- The same status lives in more than one place, and they don't always agree.
- When something goes wrong, nobody can say who changed it or when.
- Nobody is confident they could recover a mistaken deletion.
- Real decisions happen in a chat thread and never make it back into a record.
- One person is the only one who really understands the sheet.

Start with one recent disagreement about an item or an order. Trace where each version came from and decide who should own that information. You may be able to fix part of the process before replacing any software.
