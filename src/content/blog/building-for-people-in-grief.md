A small nonprofit supports women through the loss of a parent, with community events, support groups and subsidized therapy. When we started working with them, none of it ran on software. Four staff with no engineers held the whole community together through conversations and memory.

That only stretches so far as a community grows. Between events, members had no way to find each other, and staff couldn't easily see who nobody had checked in on lately.

## The front door and the back office, together

We built the public site and the members' app at the same time. A site that brings in applications isn't worth much if nobody has a good place to review them. Applications now arrive in one place, staff review them, and new members join as a group. The form asks one small set of questions at a time, since many women apply from a phone during a hard stretch.

## Somewhere to belong between events

Members can see what's coming up, register and pay for events, and join a waitlist that moves them up on its own. There's also a memorial wall where each member remembers her parent. The anniversary is kept on the Hebrew calendar and worked out fresh each year, and before the day, other members can leave her a wish.

## Four people looking after everyone

Staff can assign reach-out tasks that show who hasn't heard from anyone in a while, and see how much each person already has on their plate. Donations and receipts live in the same system. Members share a lot here, so each member's record is treated as hers: closing an account is her call, and staff preview the app with a test account that can't reach anyone real.

## What we'd tell another small nonprofit

Before you think about features, ask what your community needs between events. That's the part that's hardest to hold in anyone's head, and it's where software helps a small team most.
